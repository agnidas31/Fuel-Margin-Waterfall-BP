01

The problem

A margin number with no story behind it

When margin dropped a cent, nobody could say why without a manual investigation across systems. One cent across hundreds of millions of gallons is millions of dollars.

Fig. 1 · Where it startedillustrative

Standard margin, this month7.5 cpg▼ 1.0 cpg vs. last month · ≈ $5M
But why?Market moved?Brand premium changed?Supply terms worse?Volume vs. invoice?Environmental costs?
Every driver in a different system

Market prices by terminal, invoices by delivery, volumes by site, terms by contract.

Averages lie

A simple average gives a small site the same weight as a large one.

The parts must add up

A waterfall is only trusted if the bars sum exactly to the reported margin.

02

The approach

One grain, volume-weighted, every driver as its own bar

Store each component in dollars at site × day × grade; convert to cents per gallon only at report time, so the bars always sum to the total.

01Ingest

Market reference prices, retail prices, invoices, contract terms and volumes.

02Align

Map everything to site × day × fuel grade, with site attributes attached.

03Weight

Store components in dollars; divide by total gallons at the last step.

04Decompose

Calculate each driver so components sum exactly to the total.

05Reconcile

Check against the finance margin every refresh; watch the “others” bar.

06Report

Tableau waterfalls: monthly, YTD and year on year, from network to site.

Reading it

Illustrative month

Brand value held. Pricing at the pump wasn't the problem.

The bp premium widened by 1.1 cpg. Branded supply cost more relative to the market low.

So the conversation goes to supply, not pricing, with the size of the gap already on the table.

8.5 cpg × 500M gallons = $42.5M standard margin
